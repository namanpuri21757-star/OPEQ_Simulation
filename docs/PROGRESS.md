## Seeing the debrief

The debrief opens when the game ends, so the quickest way to see it is a short
game with the fast timers:

```
npm run demo -- --preset=test-fast --years=4
```

That prints the same five links as a normal demo. Open the instructor link and
the projector link, and the three team links if you want real decisions in it;
a country nobody claims simply repeats its starting number and is marked `auto`,
which is worth seeing in the debrief too.

1. Press the one big button through the briefing, the practice year, Years 1 to
   4 and the chat window after Year 3. Each year lasts five seconds, so you can
   let the timer close them for you.
2. In the chat window, send a promise from one team ("We will produce 38.3") and
   then have that team produce something else. The debrief marks it.
3. After the last year the big button says **End the game**, then **Reveal
   results to teams**. The dashboard then shows the **Debrief** panel.
4. Move between **World comparison**, **World detail**, **Leaderboard**, **Game
   theory** and **Questions**. Press **Show on projector** on any of them and
   watch the projector window follow in large type. **Back to the room screen**
   puts it back.
5. **Print-friendly summary** opens `/instructor/summary`: every screen, every
   world, laid out for paper with the numbers under each chart already open.
6. **Download the results** at the bottom of the debrief gives six CSV files and
   one JSON file. They are built on your own computer and need no internet.
7. **Materials** in the header opens `/instructor/materials`: the student
   briefing, the role cards, the runbook, the debrief guide and the privacy
   note, with every number taken from the settings of the game you just played.
   The same five documents are in `docs/materials/`, written by
   `npm run benchmarks` and checked by `npm run verify`.

On the team screens, the end of the game shows each group its own summary and
the three reflection questions straight away. The world chart and the
leaderboard appear only after you press **Reveal results to teams**.

## Trying a whole game on your own

You need one browser and about ten minutes. Nothing leaves your computer.

1. `npm run demo` (it builds the screens the first time). It prints five links.
2. Open the **instructor** link. That is the dashboard, and the link has your
   instructor key in it, so it is the one link to keep to yourself.
3. Open the **projector** link in another window. Put it on a second screen if
   you have one. It can only ever show what a room is allowed to see.
4. Open the three **team** links in three more windows - one per country.
   A browser remembers one country per computer, so the second and third windows
   will ask whether to use that window for the new code instead. Say yes. (In a
   classroom each group has its own computer and the question never comes up.)
   While you are playing solo, do not reload a team window: all the windows share
   one browser, so a reload comes back as whichever country joined last.
5. In the instructor window, press the one big button. It always says the next
   step: start the briefing, start the practice year, close it, start Year 1, and
   on through the chat windows, the summit and the final-year warning. `N` does
   the same thing from the keyboard and `Space` pauses.
6. In each team window, type a number and submit. The year closes when its timer
   runs out or when you press **Close now**. A country that did not submit
   repeats its last number, marked `auto`.
7. Open `/check` in any window to measure the round trip. The result appears on
   the dashboard as `Network check: 1 of 1 OK`.

Press Ctrl+C in the terminal to stop. Everything played is saved under
`data/demo`, and the dashboard comes back where it left off, paused, saying
**Resumed after restart - press Resume**.

Other things worth a look: **Print team cards** on the dashboard opens a
print-friendly page with one card per team, four to an A4 page, each with its
country, its code and a QR code for its join link. The **Advanced settings**
section on the setup screen has every setting grouped, with a live preview of
what cooperating, competing and cheating pay under the numbers you choose.
